# Phase 1 — Understand the Product

> Before a single visual: what are we actually building, and what must it *do* to people?

## 1.1 What Agentic AI Hyderabad is

A **digital city** — the home, workshop, bazaar, library and promenade for people who build, live and work with AI agents. Not a website about a city. Not a metaverse. A *navigable information space experienced like a film*, where:

- **Work happens visibly.** Agent runs are light moving through the city — builds "cook" overnight like dum biryani, sealed and slow.
- **The city keeps time.** It runs on Hyderabad's clock (IST). Dawn over the lake, blue hour over the old city, night over the Cyber Mile. Festivals change the lighting of the whole world.
- **Contribution is civic, not gamified.** You don't farm points. You earn statues, street names, citizenship, pearls — the honors a real city gives.
- **Everything is a place.** Docs are a library. The marketplace is a bazaar. Notifications are sound traveling along Golconda's walls. Search is an auto-rickshaw you hail from anywhere.

The product underneath (communities, agents, learning, marketplace) is the **ai-community** platform; the city is its interface and its identity.

## 1.2 The four questions — answered honestly

### Why would someone spend *hours* here?
Because their work lives here — not because it's pretty. Beauty gets 20 minutes; utility gets 4 hours. The city must be a workplace first:
- Agent runs are observable *as city activity* (watching your build progress is the new watching CI logs).
- Exploration is layered: the reveal, then districts, then interiors, then stories inside stories.
- The promenade (feed), the chai ritual (presence), and the city clock create ambient reasons to linger.
**Design implication:** every beautiful scene must contain a working surface within one interaction. No dead postcards.

### Why would someone share screenshots?
Because the screenshot carries a *story*:
- The reveal moment itself ("wait — this is Hyderabad") is the primary share.
- Ceremonial artifacts are designed share-first: citizenship certificate (Chatta Bazaar calligraphy register), first-build unveiling (dome lifting off a plinth), statue unveiling on Tank Bund, pearl grading cards (cut / clarity / color / carat).
- Pearl-iridescent AI artifacts look like nothing else on the internet.
**Design implication:** every ceremony outputs a beautifully composed still at native share resolution.

### Why would someone return every day?
Rituals and rhythm, not streaks:
- The city looks different at 7am, 6pm and midnight IST; monsoon mist rolls in some mornings.
- Overnight builds finish at dawn — the city literally has news every morning.
- Chai-time presence (a soft, opt-in "at chai" status), promenade strolls, festival seasons (Bonalu, Bathukamma, Sankranti kites, Ramzan night market) create living-calendar reasons.
**Design implication:** ship the clock before the features. An empty city that changes with time beats a full city frozen at noon.

### Why would someone feel *ownership*?
Civic honor, not inventory:
- Tank Bund statues for eminent contributors; streets named for founding builders; guildhalls for teams; a dignified archive (the Tombs) that honors even what is retired.
- Citizenship numbers; founding cohorts (the "1591" — our first citizens); the tehzeeb code citizens uphold together.
**Design implication:** ownership = your name in the city's stone, in its stories, and in its manners.

## 1.3 What this is NOT (Phase 1 rejections)

- **Not a museum.** Beauty that can't be touched gets ignored by day three.
- **Not a theme park.** No kitsch, no "Incredible India" pastiche, no tricolor gradients, no Charminar emoji.
- **Not a metaverse clone.** No avatar legs wandering empty plazas. Camera, not avatar.
- **Not a portfolio piece.** Every landmark must *do* something; every feature must *live* somewhere.
- **Not exclusionary.** A city is for everyone who lives in it. Postcard mode (stills, no motion) must run on low-end Android on 3G.

## 1.4 The north-star test

Every asset answers, in order:
1. Is it among the most beautiful digital product experiences anywhere? (global bar)
2. Does it reward the second look with recognition? (Hyderabad bar)
3. Does it make a Hyderabadi feel *seen*, not decorated? (local bar)
4. Would someone screenshot it without being asked? (share bar)

If 1 fails, nothing else matters. That is why Phase 6 comes last.
