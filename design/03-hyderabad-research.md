# Phase 3 — Research Hyderabad

> Forget software for a while. Understand the city as designers understand Kyoto or Rome: through materials, meanings and manners. **For every landmark: what it symbolizes → why people remember it → how it becomes a digital experience. Never "landmark picture."**

---

## 3.1 The founding story (this is the brand)

In **1591**, Sultan Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded Hyderabad as a **planned city for a new era** — tradition says at the start of a new Islamic millennium. He marked the founding with a ceremonial gateway at the crossing of the city's two great roads: the **Charminar**. The city grid radiated from it; planners like Jan Pieper read the layout as an attempt to build *a replica of paradise*. It was also, remarkably, the **first monumental building in the world of granite bonded with lime mortar** — a materials innovation — with lime plaster stabilized by organic additives (egg white, jaggery): craftsmen *formulating* better materials, four centuries before materials science.

**The parallel is exact.** Agentic AI Hyderabad is a planned city for a new era (the agentic one), marked by a ceremonial gateway, built with new materials, open to all. Hyderabad has been founded as a technology city **twice** already — 1591's planned millennial city, and 1998's Cyberabad (Cyber Towers, inaugurated November 1998, birthplace of HITEC City). We are the third founding. This story — not a landmark photo — is the identity.

**Old city / new city duality:** the 400-year-old walled city south of the Musi (Charminar, Laad Bazaar, Mecca Masjid — granite, lime, stucco, warmth, tehzeeb) versus the 30-year-old cybersecurity-grade west (HITEC City, Financial District, Kokapet supertalls — glass, speed, ambition), joined at the heart-shaped Hussain Sagar. Our digital city inherits this **duality as its architecture**: heritage quarter ↔ frontier quarter, one lake at the center of both.

## 3.3 Materials research (the actual palette of the city)

- **Deccan granite** — the structural truth. Matte, dark, dotted grey; 2.5 billion years old. *Grounds everything.*
- **Lime plaster & stucco** — the warm white of Charminar, Qutb Shahi Tombs, old-city facades; historically formulated with egg white and jaggery for durability. *Our light surfaces: warm, never sterile.*
- **Jali screens** — carved lattice: ventilation, filtered light, seeing-without-being-seen. *Our skeleton loaders and privacy filters.*
- **Bidriware** — Hyderabad's signature craft: blackened zinc-copper alloy inlaid with silver. *Our UI chrome: dark surfaces, silver hairlines.* A material language that is literally from this city and looks like precision engineering.
- **Pearls** — the city's oldest export ("City of Pearls"); ranihaar strands, soft luster. *Presence, honor, and AI-at-work: a soft iridescence, never a neon glow.*
- **Lac bangles (Laad Bazaar)** — stacked rings of color. *The one place saturated color is allowed: marketplace, festivals.*
- **Deccani miniature palettes** — lapis, malachite green, saffron, oxide red, burnished gold on buff ground. *Illustration & ceremonial accents.*
- **Telugu & Nastaliq letterforms** — Telugu's rounded curves; Nastaliq's diagonal cascade. *Ceremonial typography only, per the Chatta Bazaar rule.*
- **The 10° tilt** — the old city's grid is rotated ~10° off true north. *Optional signature: our data-viz baselines tilt 10° — locals will never consciously see it; designers will eventually find it and scream.*

## 3.4 Emotional geography (how the city *feels*)

- **Tehzeeb:** unhurried courtesy; the pause before speech; no one is rushed and no one is ignored.
- **Nawabi scale:** grandeur expressed in *proportion and calm*, not loudness.
- **Bazaar energy:** maximum density, minimum anxiety — rhythm and repetition (arches, stalls, bangle rings) make chaos legible.
- **Lake stillness:** the whole city orients around one calm center.
- **Deep time:** granite beneath everything; a city that has outlived empires builds for centuries.

These five feelings are the **emotional spec**. Phase 4 turns them into laws.
